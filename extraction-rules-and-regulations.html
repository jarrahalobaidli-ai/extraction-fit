I.

Chain of Command

1.1

Commander (Coach)

Reviews every MAYDAY Report, assigns the operator's Manual, approves phase advancement, and monitors incoming SITREPs. Final call on load, progression, and any deviation from the standard timeline.

1.2

Operator (Client)

Executes the assigned Manual as written, reports status on schedule, and flags equipment gaps, missed sessions, or pain — not normal fatigue — the moment they happen, not at the next check-in.

II.

Mission Timeline

Every engagement runs the same architecture regardless of tier. Phases are not optional and are not skipped — each one sets up the load the next phase depends on.

Phase 0 · Days 1–3
Mayday
Intake report submitted, equipment confirmed, baseline Manual assigned.
Weeks 1–3
Designate Your Heading
Baseline established. Form standards locked in before load increases.
Weeks 4–7
Golden Hour
Progressive overload begins. Volume and complexity step up on schedule.
Weeks 8–11
Hold the Line
Peak intensity window — the highest demand block of the cycle.
Weeks 12–13
RTB
Deload, retest against baseline, Return‑to‑Base debrief.
2.1

Sustained Ops

After RTB, the operator either re-enters at DESIGNATE YOUR HEADING for a new cycle, steps up a tier, or moves to Sustained Ops maintenance cadence. Standing still is the one option off the table.

III.

Comms Protocol

3.1

Ops Freq

WhatsApp is the primary channel for both sides — intake questions, SITREPs, and anything time-sensitive. It is not for scheduling live calls in the self-guided Extraction Protocol tier.

Extraction ProtocolRecovery TeamDirect Action
3.2

SITREP

An async text-and-photo check-in on the cadence agreed at MAYDAY — not a scheduled call. Live check-ins are a Recovery Team / Direct Action feature and are booked separately from Ops Freq.

3.3

Response window

Expect a reply within one business day, not in real time. For anything urgent — pain, injury, an equipment failure mid-Manual — say so plainly in the message; it moves to the front of the queue.

IV.

Conduct & Commitment

4.1

Full term, not a trial

Commitment is to the full cycle, not an optional spend evaluated week to week. Results come from finishing MAYDAY through RTB, not from the first two good weeks.

4.2

Report, don't ghost

A missed session gets reported on Ops Freq, ideally rescheduled inside the same week. A pattern of unreported misses is treated as a SITREP flag, not silently ignored.

4.3

Form over ego

No loading past clean form, especially through GOLDEN HOUR and HOLD THE LINE as weight climbs. Form-check video on request is not optional — it's how load increases stay safe.

4.4

Pain pauses, it doesn't cancel

Report pain — as distinct from normal training fatigue — the moment it shows up. Commander adjusts the Manual around it. Working through it silently is the one thing that actually ends a cycle early.

V.

Manuals & Progression

5.1

Assignment

Every operator is assigned a Manual from the library after MAYDAY intake, matched to equipment access and current phase. Manuals are the deliverable — no operator trains without one on file.

5.2

Phase advancement

Phase moves forward on the standard schedule (DESIGNATE YOUR HEADING → GOLDEN HOUR → HOLD THE LINE → RTB) unless SITREPs indicate otherwise. Commander can hold a phase; an operator cannot self-advance.

5.3

Edits happen at phase boundaries

Manual swaps and edits land between phases, not mid-week — the training stimulus stays consistent that way. Equipment loss or injury are the standing exceptions and get actioned immediately.

VI.

RTB Debrief

6.1

Retest

Every RTB retests the same markers set at MAYDAY. The comparison — not a feeling — is what closes the cycle out.

6.2

Field Log closes

Progress data and photos from the cycle are compiled into the operator's Field Log at RTB, before the next decision gets made.

6.3

The decision point

RTB ends with one of three calls: re-enter at DESIGNATE YOUR HEADING for a new cycle, step up a tier, or move to Sustained Ops. Commander recommends; the operator decides.

Acknowledged & Accepted

By continuing past MAYDAY intake, the operator confirms they've read this SOP and agree to run the mission by it — Commander included.
