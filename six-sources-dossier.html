Seven Sources

Seven brands already doing what the Extraction shop is trying to do — sell identity, not just gear. This is what each one actually does well, and the specific moves worth stealing for Patches, Apparel, and Grooming.

01

The Seven

Seven brands built a merch line that people wear as an identity, not a souvenir. Here's the one thing each one does that Extraction doesn't do yet.

SOURCE 01

Til Valhalla Project

Cause-Anchored Apparel

Founded by a Marine who started hand-making memorial plaques for fallen friends' families before he ever sold a shirt. The apparel exists to fund the plaques — the product is downstream of the mission, not the other way around.

Borrow: give one product a real cause behind it, not just a design behind it.
SOURCE 02

Grunt Style

Patriotic Lifestyle Brand

Started by a former Drill Sergeant with $1,200. Built a huge, almost overwhelming catalog — bundles, state-specific drops, seasonal lines — but the constant underneath it all is a simple loyalty pledge that has nothing to do with rank or unit.

Borrow: catalog breadth works once there's one loyalty idea holding it together.
SOURCE 03

Jocko Fuel

Performance Nutrition

Every product, from pre-workout to a kids' protein shake, sits under one three-word philosophy line (quoted below). Jocko didn't build a supplement brand and add a slogan — he built a slogan and hung a supplement line off it.

Borrow: one phrase, repeated everywhere, outperforms ten good taglines.
SOURCE 04

Jocko Store

Podcast-Driven Merch

Runs a monthly mystery-shirt subscription (the "Shurt Locker") alongside the normal catalog — people pay to be surprised by a brand they already trust. It converts one-time buyers into a recurring line item.

Borrow: a recurring drop box turns merch from a purchase into a habit.
SOURCE 05

The Drive Clothing

Motivational Streetwear

One skull mark, stretched across an enormous accessory catalog — mugs, koozies, beard products, decals, even unmarked "mystery boxes." The core symbol barely changes; what changes is how many surfaces it's printed on.

Borrow: low-cost accessories and grab-bags are where volume lives, not the flagship tee.
SOURCE 06

All American Roughneck

Blue-Collar Identity Wear

Seth Feroce's line brands its entire audience with one blunt, shared acronym printed across hats, hoodies, and stickers — a shorthand members can flash at each other. Seasonal drops (spring, summer, winter) keep the acronym feeling current instead of stale.

Borrow: a members-only shorthand does more tribal work than a logo.
SOURCE 07

Zero Foxtrot

Veteran-Founded Military Lifestyle

Veteran-founded, and the only one of the seven that doesn't lean on olive drab or black-and-red — it pairs dark backgrounds with an unexpected purple accent, which reads as premium rather than tacticool cliché. Collections get their own names ("Namakubi") instead of generic category labels, and a paid membership club sits alongside a one-off collectible novelty item styled like a laminated ID or dog-tag card. The tone stays dark-humored but never disrespectful.

Borrow: a named collection and a small collectible card do more for repeat visits than another t-shirt design.
02

Pattern Extracted

Strip away the six different aesthetics and the same four decisions keep showing up. None of these brands are selling a t-shirt — they're selling a reason to belong to something, and the shirt is just the receipt.

First, every one of them picked a single phrase or symbol and refused to dilute it. Jocko has one line. The Drive has one skull. Grunt Style has one pledge. The temptation with a growing catalog is to keep inventing new taglines — the brands that actually stick did the opposite.

Second, the highest-margin, highest-volume items are almost never the flagship piece. It's the $12 accessory — the patch, the sticker, the mug, the mystery box — that a member buys on impulse and that a $45 hoodie never will.

Third, recurring beats one-off. A subscription box or a seasonal drop calendar gives people a reason to come back to the store instead of buying once and disappearing.

Fourth, the brand's own story — a founder's loss, a veteran workforce, a hard-labor identity — is doing as much selling as the graphic design. None of these are just print shops with a mission statement bolted on; the mission came first.

"Discipline Equals Freedom" Jocko Fuel / Jocko Willink — one phrase run across an entire product line

Build Orders

Eight concrete moves for the Extraction shop, each one traceable to a specific source above and to something Extraction already has on the site.

01

Give "Saved Others" a real cause

The CSAR-tribute patch already exists. Attach a fixed portion of every sale to something real — a SAR family fund, cadet gear, a first-responder cause — and it stops being a novelty patch and becomes the one product people buy on principle, not impulse.

From Source 01 — Til Valhalla
02

Pick one flagship phrase and stop splitting focus

Right now "No One Is Coming," "Disciplined," and "Sharpened" are three separate concepts competing for attention. The site's own footer line — "Extraction // From Mediocrity // No One Is Coming" — is already the strongest one on the page. Make that the phrase that runs across the patch, the tee, a mug, and a hat, the way Jocko runs one line across everything.

From Source 03 — Jocko Fuel
03

Launch an Extraction Crate

A quarterly drop box timed to the coaching program's own phase language (a Foundation batch, an Intensity batch) bundling a patch, a small accessory, and one surprise item. Turns a single sale into a standing subscription line the way Jocko's Shurt Locker does.

From Source 04 — Jocko Store
04

Coin one members-only mark

A short shorthand built off something already on the site — "SIX," or a mark tied to "No One Is Coming" — printed small on everything, the way HWM works for All American Roughneck. It should read as an inside reference, not a slogan.

From Source 06 — All American Roughneck
05

Add a low-cost "Blackout Box"

An unmarked grab-bag of patches at a flat low price, the way The Drive runs mystery boxes. Cheap enough to be an impulse add-on at checkout once checkout exists, and it moves inventory on designs that aren't anyone's first pick alone.

From Source 05 — The Drive Clothing
06

Run drops on a calendar, not all at once

Time releases to something already named on the site — a "Sustained Ops" quarterly collection, matching the existing Events badge — instead of shipping the whole catalog in one pass. Gives people a reason to check back.

From Source 06 — All American Roughneck
07

Add a small family line

The original brief was "build community, not just operators." A lightweight partner or kids' size of the flagship tee — the way AAR runs a dedicated women's line — puts that intent into an actual product instead of just copy on the shop page.

From Source 06 — All American Roughneck
08

Ship a clearance-card collectible with every order

A cheap laminated-ID-style "Extraction Clearance Card" — the skull-in-crosshair mark, an operator number, "CLEARANCE VERIFIED" — dropped into every order the way Zero Foxtrot ships its collectible cards. Costs almost nothing to produce and gives every buyer something to photograph and post.

From Source 07 — Zero Foxtrot
